A VPN (Virtual Private Network) is a tool that creates a secure, encrypted connection between your device and the internet. It basically acts like a private tunnel for your data. Here’s how it works step by step: 1. You connect to a VPN server When you turn on a VPN (using apps like NordVPN or ExpressVPN or surfsharkVPN or protonVPN), your device connects to a remote server operated by the VPN provider.

2. Your data gets encrypted Before your data leaves your device, the VPN encrypts it. That means: Your internet provider can’t see what you’re doing Hackers on public Wi-Fi can’t read your data Encryption turns your data into unreadable code until it reaches the VPN server.

3. Your IP address is hidden Normally, websites see your real IP address (which reveals your location). With a VPN: Websites see the VPN server’s IP instead Your real location stays hidden For example, if you connect to a server in Germany, it looks like you’re browsing from Germany—even if you’re in Slovenia.

4. The VPN server accesses the internet for you The VPN server sends your request to the website (like Google or YouTube), receives the response, and sends it back to you through the encrypted tunnel. Simple analogy Think of it like mailing a letter:

Without VPN: you send it directly with your home address on it With VPN: you put it in a secure envelope and send it through a middleman who forwards it—so the recipient only sees the middleman’s address What a VPN is useful for Privacy: hides your activity from your ISP Security: protects you on public Wi-Fi Access: lets you view region-restricted content Anonymity (partial): makes tracking harder (but not impossible) What a VPN does not do It doesn’t make you fully anonymous It doesn’t protect you from viruses or scams You still need to trust the VPN provider (they could log your data)
